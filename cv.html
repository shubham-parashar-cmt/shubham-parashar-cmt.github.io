Research Profile

Theoretical physicist developing controlled analytical and computational descriptions of transport in spatially inhomogeneous two-dimensional electron systems. My work connects semiclassical Boltzmann kinetics, electron hydrodynamics, magnetotransport, quantum Hall response, and finite-resolution inverse problems. Recent projects derive the higher-harmonic obstruction to hydrodynamic closure, formulate observability tests for scanning-probe images, and apply stochastic worldline methods to heavy-tailed trajectory geometry.

Research Areas

Quantum transport; electron hydrodynamics; semiclassical kinetic theory; magnetotransport in graphene and two-dimensional electron gases; quantum Hall thermoelectric and viscous response; inverse problems and observability; nonequilibrium stochastic dynamics and rare events.

Selected Research Contributions

  • Derived the analytical and numerical magnetotransport response of smooth density perturbations across Ohmic and hydrodynamic regimes, including magnetic-field-dependent current suppression, Landauer resistivity dipoles, and Gurzhi-scale redistribution.
  • Developed semiclassical Boltzmann calculations used to interpret graphene scanning-tunneling potentiometry near a circular p–n junction, connecting diffusive spiraling flow to nonlocal cyclotron-scale structure.
  • Identified the fourth-order current operator omitted by stress-level hydrodynamic closure, derived its coefficient from the lowest discarded Fermi-surface harmonic, and tracked its chiral finite-wave-number completion to a collisionless Bessel pole–zero ladder.
  • Formulated a finite-resolution transport-functional framework for quantum Hall nanoscopy, including a thermoelectric Hall contrast relation and covariance-weighted nuisance projection for Hall-viscous inference.
  • Recast denominator-tail exponents as Mellin poles of worldline composite operators; derived exact optimal-fluctuation prefactors, a diffusion-defect mechanism that shifts the exponent, and finite-memory conditions that protect the smooth-law index.
