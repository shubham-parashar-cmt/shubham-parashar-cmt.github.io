P. Shubham Parashar

Quantum transport in two-dimensional electron systems

I develop kinetic, hydrodynamic, and inverse-observability methods for spatially resolved transport. The central question is how finite-resolution measurements of voltage, current, and heat distinguish the electronic dynamics that produced them.

Research

Controlled transport theory for spatial measurements

Magnetotransport and electron hydrodynamics

Current flow around smooth density inhomogeneities in two-dimensional electron systems, from Ohmic and viscous transport to nonlocal cyclotron-scale response. This work includes Landauer resistivity dipoles, magnetic-field-induced current suppression, and Gurzhi-scale redistribution.

Kinetic closures and chiral nonlocality

Controlled elimination of higher Fermi-surface harmonics. Recent work identifies the fourth-order operator omitted by stress-level hydrodynamic closure and follows its magnetic-field completion from a low-gradient coefficient to collisionless pole–zero structure.

Quantum Hall scanning-probe observability

What can a finite-resolution image actually determine? I formulate thermoelectric Hall contrast relations and covariance-aware tests that separate Hall-viscous response from boundary, electrothermal, kinetic, and higher-gradient nuisance channels.
