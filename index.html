My research develops analytical and computational descriptions of transport in spatially inhomogeneous two-dimensional electron systems. It connects semiclassical Boltzmann kinetics, electron hydrodynamics, magnetotransport, quantum Hall response, and finite-resolution inverse problems. I also study stochastic worldline mechanisms and heavy-tailed trajectory geometry.

Research overview

Spatial magnetotransport and kinetic theory

I study current flow around density inhomogeneities in two-dimensional electron systems across diffusive, hydrodynamic, and ballistic regimes. Current work examines where stress-level hydrodynamic closure fails, how higher angular harmonics enter magnetotransport, and how chiral finite-wave-number response crosses into the nonlocal regime.

Quantum Hall scanning-probe response

I ask which transport coefficients can actually be inferred from finite-resolution images of voltage, current, and heat. This work develops thermoelectric Hall contrast relations and controlled finite-band tests of Hall-viscous response in the presence of boundary and higher-gradient nuisance contributions.

Worldline fluctuations and trajectory statistics

I study how endpoint laws, diffusion defects, and finite observation windows alter heavy tails in trajectory geometry. A related collaboration investigates small-denominator mechanisms for extreme deviation angle, curvature, and torsion in light inertial-particle trajectories.
