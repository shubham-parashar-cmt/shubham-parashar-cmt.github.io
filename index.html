Email · CV · Google Scholar · ORCID · GitHub

I develop controlled analytical and computational descriptions of transport in spatially inhomogeneous two-dimensional electron systems. A central question is which microscopic transport mechanisms can be distinguished from finite-resolution measurements. My work connects semiclassical Boltzmann kinetics, electron hydrodynamics, magnetotransport, quantum Hall response, and quantum-transport calculations. I also study stochastic mechanisms behind heavy-tailed trajectory geometry.

Research overview

From kinetic theory to hydrodynamics

I derive spatial transport response around controlled density inhomogeneities and test the limits of low-moment hydrodynamic closures. I also use symmetry-selected drives to construct local hydrodynamic null tests whose leading signal is kinetic, exposing higher angular harmonics that are invisible to stress-level closure. The aim is to identify which angular harmonics and nonlocal terms survive in experimentally accessible voltage, current, and dissipation maps.

Finite-resolution transport inference

I study what scanning-probe measurements can identify, rather than only which bulk coefficients a theory contains. This work develops thermoelectric Hall contrast relations and finite-band tests of Hall-viscous response in the presence of boundary and higher-gradient contributions.

Trajectory-geometry tails

I use worldline optimal fluctuations to determine how endpoint laws, diffusion defects, crossovers, and finite observation windows alter heavy tails in geometric observables. A related collaboration studies small-denominator mechanisms for extreme deviation angle, curvature, and torsion in light inertial-particle trajectories.
