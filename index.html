I study how microscopic kinetic structure appears in spatial transport measurements, and where hydrodynamic descriptions cease to be complete. My work connects semiclassical Boltzmann kinetics, electron hydrodynamics, magnetotransport, quantum Hall response, and finite-resolution inference in two-dimensional electron systems.

Research

Current program

Beyond hydrodynamic closure

In a hydrodynamic description, only a few slow moments of the electron distribution are retained. I derive the higher-gradient and finite-wave-number response beyond stress-level closure, including a fourth-order closure obstruction and its chiral nonlocal completion.

arXiv:2607.22730 ↗

Symmetry-selected kinetic probes

The symmetry of the drive can force the dominant heating channels to vanish at one point in a device, leaving a weaker kinetic effect as the leading local signal. Under a sixfold drive, Ohmic and rank-two viscous dissipation vanish there while a higher angular harmonic survives.

Finite-resolution transport inference

Spatial probes have finite resolution, so not every transport coefficient is identifiable from a measured image. I test identifiability of thermoelectric Hall contrast and Hall-viscous response once resolution, boundaries, and higher-gradient nuisance terms enter.

arXiv:2607.16315 ↗

Related statistical-physics work

Rare-event and small-denominator mechanisms behind heavy-tailed trajectory geometry, including endpoint conditions, crossovers, and finite observation windows.
