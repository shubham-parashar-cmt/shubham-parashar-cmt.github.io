P. Shubham Parashar

Theoretical condensed matter physicist

Independent researcher · San Diego, California

My research concerns spatially resolved transport in quantum materials: how local measurements of voltage, current, and heat can be used to identify the underlying electronic dynamics. I also work on statistical questions in inertial-particle turbulence.

Research overview

Spatial magnetotransport and electron hydrodynamics

I study current flow around density inhomogeneities in two-dimensional electron systems. My published work treats the diffusive, hydrodynamic, and ballistic regimes in magnetic fields, including Landauer resistivity dipoles, current-suppressed regions, and cyclotron-scale structure measured by scanning tunneling potentiometry.

Quantum Hall scanning-probe response

My submitted work asks which transport coefficients can actually be identified from finite-resolution images. It develops thermoelectric Hall contrast relations and a controlled finite-band analysis of Hall-viscous response in the presence of boundary and higher-gradient nuisance contributions.

Trajectory statistics in turbulence

My work in preparation on turbulence concerns the heavy-tailed geometry of light inertial-particle trajectories. The central idea is that large deviation angle, curvature, and torsion arise from small-denominator events whose exponents are fixed by local phase-space geometry.
